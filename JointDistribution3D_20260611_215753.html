What it is

The CRR™ 3D Joint Distribution is a three-dimensional probability surface showing the likelihood of every combination of failure frequency and per-event cost. Height at any point encodes probability density — how probable that (k, cost) outcome is.

Built from the exact analytical compound Poisson–lognormal density, evaluated on a 120×160 vertex grid, rendered in real time via WebGL (Three.js r128). This is the closed-form joint density, not a simulation trace.

Core question answered

Not just “what is my expected loss?” but “what is the full shape of the risk?” — how fat the tails are, where the mass concentrates, and which frequency–severity combinations drive the worst outcomes.

The statistical model

CRR™ models portfolio risk as a compound Poisson process: event count per year is Poisson-distributed; per-event cost is independently lognormal. Their product is the annual aggregate loss.

Poisson frequency
P(N = k) = (λk · e−λ) / k! λ = 76.052 events/year

Most years see 60–92 events, with tails extending in both directions.

Lognormal severity
f(x) = [1/(xσ√2π)] · exp(−(ln x − μ)² / 2σ²)
μ = 12.1942  ·  σ = 0.6488  ·  CV = 0.7234

Median per-event cost: e12.1942 ≈ $198,500. CV = 0.7234 signals meaningful severity volatility.

Joint density
f(k, x) = P(N=k) · fLN(x | μ, σ) · |∂x/∂(axis)|

The Jacobian term keeps visual mass correctly distributed when toggling log vs. linear axis modes.

Log vs. linear axis

Log mode compresses the cost axis so the lognormal looks symmetric. Linear mode shows the true right-skewed shape. Same density; only axis scaling changes.

Skirt panels

All four perimeter edges drop vertical skirt panels to y=0, confirming that density outside the plotted window is negligible.

The surface

A 120×160 vertex grid. Geometry is built once; heights and colors update on axis-mode or style changes without rebuilding indices.

1
Density evaluationPoisson PMF at interpolated k × lognormal PDF at cost = raw density per cell.
2
NormalizationAll densities ÷ global max (fMax) × TARGET_H = 40. Peak always the same visual height.
3
Rise animationSurface rises from ground over 110 frames (cubic ease-out), making the peak legible from the first frame.
4
Vertex normalsRecomputed after every geometry update, driving Phong, PBR, and toon shading across all 17 styles.

All 17 rendering styles share identical geometry. Only materials, colormaps, and lighting change. Thermal/Infrared and Blueprint read best on projected screens.

HUD & risk badge

The risk badge classifies the dominant driver using two thresholds:

λCVBadge
<5<0.75Frequency — reduce event rate
<5≥0.75Severity — reduce consequence
≥5<0.75Frequency — high volume, consistent costs
≥5≥0.75Both — dual exposure

This portfolio: λ = 76.052 ≥ 5, CV = 0.7234 < 0.75 → Frequency. Reducing event rate moves EAL more than reducing per-event cost.

Why it matters
1
The spread is not symmetricThe top-view contour map shows iso-loss curves bunching on the low-cost side and spreading apart on the high side — tail losses accumulate faster than the mean implies.
2
EAL vs. P50 reveals skewEAL ($18.59M) sits above P50 ($18.43M). In a symmetric distribution they would be equal. The gap is the actuarial cost of the right tail.
3
TVaR quantifies what P95 doesn’tP95 says you won’t exceed $23.6M in 19 of 20 years. TVaR95 ($25.0M) says in the worst 5% of years you’ll average $25M. TVaR is the reserve number.
4
Slice enables intervention analysisHolding k fixed and watching severity answers: “If I reduce event rate by 10%, how does the loss distribution shift?” — the trade-off that justifies OpEx investment.
iARM™ framework alignment

This visualization implements the iARM™ mission: quantify cost risk in language that financial decision-makers recognize. A 3D probability surface with labeled percentiles and TVaR bands translates directly into reserve justification and capital allocation — without reducing risk to a single misleading average.
